      How the score is built — nothing hidden

      SignalPointsWhy it counts
      Specific number or statistic+15Checkable quantities can be verified or falsified.
      Date or year given+10Anchors the claim in time; stale claims can be caught.
      Named source / citation+10“According to X” gives you somewhere to check.
      Calibrated hedging (“may”, “likely”, “suggests”)+10Honest uncertainty is a sign the speaker knows the limits.
      Absolute words (“always”, “never”, “proves”, “guaranteed”)−15Reality rarely deals in absolutes; overclaiming is a red flag.
      Weasel attribution (“experts say”, “studies show”)−10Authority with no name cannot be checked.
      Hype words (“miracle”, “revolutionary”, “best ever”)−10Marketing language crowds out evidence.
      Too short to check (< 6 words)−5A bare assertion carries no evidence at all.
      Number/statistic with no source or date to check−10A precise figure with nowhere to check it is a claim, not evidence.

      Base score 50, clamped 0–100. Verdicts: ≥ 70 Solid · 40–69 Shaky · < 40 Unverifiable. ClaimLens is a screening tool, not a fact-checker: it measures how checkable and how overclaimed a statement is. It makes no web requests and calls no model — everything runs in your browser, and nothing you paste leaves your device.
